In 1775, thirteen colonies clung to the Atlantic seaboard — prosperous, quarrelsome, and subject to a king an ocean away. Taxed without consent and garrisoned without invitation, they raised militias, then an army, and at last a Declaration: that these colonies were, and of right ought to be, free and independent states. Eight years of war would decide whether those words held. Scroll to follow it — from the first shots outside Boston to the surrender of Lord Cornwallis at Yorktown.

Finis Coronat Opus

The guns fell silent after Yorktown. Two years later, by the Treaty of Paris (1783), Great Britain acknowledged the United States of America to be free, sovereign, and independent — and the last redcoats sailed out of New York Harbor. A map of colonies had become a map of a nation.
